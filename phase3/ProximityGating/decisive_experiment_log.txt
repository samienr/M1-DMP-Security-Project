Decisive confound experiment: does DMP activation depend on data_buffer's
absolute address, or on distance between aop and data_buffer?

All runs: ./bin/augury 256 1 32 4 1 <data_buffer_addr> <aop_addr>
(aop forcing added to augury.c this session; previously only data_buffer
could be forced.)

=== Test 1: both forced close together (16 MiB apart), deep in the
    Augury paper's documented "dead zone" (aop=0x200000000, ~8 GiB) ===
trial 1: train_speedup=3.973 test_speedup=2.785
trial 2: train_speedup=4.213 test_speedup=2.233
trial 3: train_speedup=3.966 test_speedup=1.318
trial 4: train_speedup=3.977 test_speedup=3.372
trial 5: train_speedup=3.881 test_speedup=5.028
trial 6: train_speedup=4.094 test_speedup=3.862
trial 7: train_speedup=4.187 test_speedup=5.321
trial 8: train_speedup=3.994 test_speedup=3.266
-> 8/8 strong activation (all train_speedup 3.88-4.21x), despite both
   buffers being deep in the region the Augury paper says the DMP will
   never dereference.

=== Test 2 (control): same dead-zone region, but far apart
    (aop=0x200000000, data_buffer=0x400000000, ~8 GiB separation) ===
trial 1: train_speedup=1.007 test_speedup=1.020
trial 2: train_speedup=0.999 test_speedup=0.981
trial 3: train_speedup=1.001 test_speedup=1.019
trial 4: train_speedup=1.000 test_speedup=0.980
trial 5: train_speedup=0.996 test_speedup=1.000
-> 5/5 no activation (all ~1.0x) -- same absolute region as Test 1,
   only the separation changed. Confirms it's proximity, not just
   "forcing aop's address via MAP_FIXED changes something."

=== Test 3: different dead-zone location, close together
    (aop=0x600000000, data_buffer=0x601000000, 16 MiB apart, ~24 GiB) ===
trial 1: train_speedup=3.879 test_speedup=4.682
trial 2: train_speedup=4.041 test_speedup=3.356
trial 3: train_speedup=4.106 test_speedup=9.391
trial 4: train_speedup=4.249 test_speedup=3.525
trial 5: train_speedup=4.292 test_speedup=5.392
-> 5/5 strong activation at a second, independent low address, ruling
   out 0x200000000 being special.

Conclusion: activation tracks AoP-to-data_buffer distance, not either
buffer's absolute virtual address. See reach_results.csv/reach_results2.csv
for the quantified falloff curve (full strength to ~3 GiB, falloff
3.1-3.8 GiB, dead beyond ~4 GiB), and gradient_results2.csv for the
earlier (unforced-aop) experiment whose apparent bimodal/probabilistic
pattern this fully explains in hindsight.
